Turn Wael into one reusable 3D guide

The Wael game app should treat 3D as one coherent illustration layer: a stylised, soft-matte 3D caricature of Wael with a small set of poses on one fixed canvas, plus the floating town. That layer sits on top of the flat, lipped play UI that already exists. It should not be real-time 3D, and the chrome should not be claymorphic. The evidence points the same way. Pedagogical agents bring a small learning benefit (g ≈ 0.20), 2D agents beat 3D agents (g+ ≈ 0.38 vs ≈ 0.11), and the persona benefit comes from gestures and facial expressions, not rendering fidelity (Castro-Alonso et al. 2021; Davis, Park & Vincent 2021). So the budget should go into Wael's reactions (a dozen poses that change with each choice) rather than more polygons. Keep the rendering deliberately stylised, because Duolingo itself called a fully textured 3D Duo "creepier" (Design Week) and Gen Z punishes anything that looks AI-made (Klaviyo). For this Vite + React stack in a webview, the recommended technology path runs cheapest first: pre-rendered WebP stills composited with CSS (almost no JavaScript) for every screen now, stacked-alpha video for two or three hero gestures, a Rive puppet only once Wael speaks with real audio, and no React Three Fiber or Spline anywhere in the flow. The rebuilt splash keeps the project's "a door, not a wait" stance: a flat launch colour, Wael on the end side facing the lime CTA in the right-thumb zone with the island behind him, an entrance under 600 ms, one ambient loop, and sound on the first tap. The same Wael then carries through welcome, level, subject, quiz, result and sign-up by swapping a single component (WaelAside). This plan requires the owner to approve seven amendments to the wael-gamified skill (listed in section 1). The weakest evidence covers mid-range Android performance, teen and MENA reactions to mascots, and community opinion, because Reddit, X, LinkedIn and YouTube were unreadable to the research tools.

2. Stylised, reactive and facing the content: principles for a 3D teacher

Faces and gestures earn the benefit, not polygons

The research gives a small, reliable learning effect and a larger liking effect. The multimedia-agent meta-analysis (32 effect sizes, N = 2104) found g ≈ 0.20 overall, with 2D agents (g+ ≈ 0.38) well ahead of 3D agents (g+ ≈ 0.11) (Castro-Alonso et al. 2021). Gesturing agents produce a persona effect of g ≈ 0.44 (Davis 2018). Facial expressions and gestures moderate persona more than voice or agent type does (Davis, Park & Vincent 2021). Faces added to learning graphics help learning and enjoyment (Brom, Stárková & D'Mello 2018; Smart Learning Environments 2024). Agent effects shrink with age, being larger for K-12 than for post-secondary learners (Schroeder, Adesope & Gilbert 2013). For 15–19-year-olds, sell Wael as an engagement and emotional-safety device, not as a score booster.

The strongest steelman against a 3D Wael is the 2D-beats-3D finding itself. The answer is that "3D" in that literature mostly meant more realistic agents. A soft, caricatured, consistently lit render that behaves like a 2D mascot (few poses, crisp silhouette, reactions) keeps the 2D virtues while meeting the owner's wish for volume. Duolingo's experience matches: the team moved away from an iOS-7-era fully textured 3D Duo it found "creepier", toward a flatter, more expressive and more scalable owl (Design Week; Creative Bloq). One report links animating Duo to about a 5 % rise in sessions, but its original source could not be re-opened, so treat it as unverified.

Synthetic presenters that are realistic hold test scores level but lose engagement. Studies report "low video engagement", plus discomfort and disconnection (IRRODL 2024), a slight preference for human video in a 447-person study (arXiv 2412.10384), and students who "strongly refrain from the synthetic avatar" (ICERI 2023). A caricature of a real teacher is safer than a near-photoreal double.

What makes a character worth its cost: it reacts

Duolingo built its characters as a state machine, not as more static art. Idle loops (nods, blinks, brows) run alongside 20+ personality-styled mouth shapes, and right and wrong answers each have an end state, all in one Rive file (Duolingo: world character visemes). Lily's video call combines 8 head and 8 body clips into more than 64 idle variations, so she never loops visibly. The file stays under 1 MB. When testers found her wide-open eyes "too happy" for her deadpan persona, the team dialled them back (Rive: Lily video call). That gives two rules:

  • Give Wael an expression ceiling matched to his real temperament.
  • Spend animation on reactions to the student's state.

Duolingo's widget shows the same principle in still form: Duo's mood illustrations follow time of day and streak status, and widget users retained much better (Duolingo: widget). Silhouette came first in Duo's design: "if the overall silhouette isn't appealing, the details can't complement it" (Duolingo: Reshaping Duo). Rewards, not characters, were Duolingo's entry point into Rive (Rive: creative technologists). Elsewhere, Khan Academy Kids' Kodi guides and celebrates through body language (Khan Kids help; Khan Academy blog). Finch creates ownership by having users hatch and name a pet (screensdesign: Finch). Royal Match's King Robert is "barely a character", more of a marketing hook (orcz review).

The Clippy line: presence must be skippable

The Clippy concept was sound, but its invasiveness drove people away. The lesson drawn is to "optimize for repeated use, not just first time use" (Command AI; TwentyBN; Nextgov). Clash Royale lets players mute an opponent's emotes (itechguides). Guilt mechanics backfire publicly: Duolingo's "sick" icon worried users, and the "dead Duo" stunt drew criticism alongside its 1.7 billion impressions (Creative Bloq; MediaPost; Fast Company). For baccalauréat students under stress, Wael never looks desperate, never guilt-trips, and can be muted or skipped. That matches the existing « مبعد » choice, which carries no guilt copy.

Gaze and gesture steer attention, so point Wael at the work

Gaze-cueing studies find that a face looking toward a product raises attention to it and memory for it, while mutual gaze pulls attention onto the face (Palcu et al. 2017; Sajjacholapunt & Ball 2014; Hutton & Nolte 2011). One thesis dissents (Univie thesis). In content screens, Wael's head and eyes turn toward the question card, the tiles or the CTA. Direct eye contact is reserved for the greeting (splash, welcome) and the celebration (result, sign-up complete), where Wael himself is the message.

For gestures in the Arab world, avoid index-finger pointing (widely considered rude) and use the open hand (Irish Times; Wikivoyage: Gestures). Sources conflict on the thumbs-up (Wikipedia: Obscene gesture), and the OK sign can read as "zero" or as an insult (englishtoarabic guide). The standard Wael vocabulary is therefore:

  • open palm to present
  • both hands raised to celebrate
  • hand on chin to think
  • gentle shrug for "not quite"

Thumbs-up waits until Tunisian teens have been tested.

Tone for 15–19: dry, warm, peer-like

The "childish" risk for teenagers comes from tone, not volume: baby talk, constant cheering, guilt. Duolingo's Gen Z lead says the audience "wants to be entertained, not sold to" (business.com interview; HubSpot). Lily's sarcasm shows that a cartoon can be "cool" through attitude (Duolingo fandom: Lily; duoplanet). Wael's voice should stay closer to Lily than to Kodi: Tunisian derja, verbs first, at most one exclamation.

RTL: re-pose, never mirror

Platform guidance agrees on most of it. Mirror the layout, navigation, progress direction and depictions of forward motion. Do not flip logos, clocks, real-world objects or general artwork: Apple says "Avoid flipping images like photographs, illustrations, and general artwork" (Apple HIG: Right to left; Material M1: Bidirectionality; Material M2; Wikimedia Codex). Some illustrations "may require a version for each direction" (Cloudscape). Material says a walking figure faces left in RTL. Sources disagree on media controls: Wikimedia mirrors them, while Material, Mozilla, Microsoft and Adobe do not (Codex v1.18; Mozilla; Microsoft Learn; Adobe Spectrum).

For Wael this means: each "present" or "point" pose ships as a left-facing and a right-facing render, chosen by layout side, never by CSS scaleX(-1). Mirroring would reverse his parted beard, a watch, a writing hand or any text on a prop. In /mobilegame the screen is always dir="rtl" and Wael lives on the end (left) side, so the right-facing set comes first. The left-facing set is only needed for LTR layouts such as a French /webgame.

Consent, authorship and the "AI look"

Three non-visual risks matter more than any frame rate.

Consent. A person's likeness is protected by privacy and publicity rights, not copyright (rightofpublicityroadmap; Jenner & Block; Authors Alliance). Get Wael's written consent to the caricature and its uses.

Authorship. Purely AI-generated art is not copyrightable in the US. Prompting alone is not authorship, and only human post-generation edits are registrable (Carlton Fields; Mondaq: Thaler; Mondaq: Allen v. Perlmutter; Troutman). Keep a log of human cleanup and edits for the brand asset. Tunisian law was not researched.

The AI look. Gen Z is about 28 % more likely than average to lose trust in brands using AI content, and 41 % trust brands less when AI is visible (Klaviyo). 73 % of US teens want AI images labelled (TechCrunch). Young people's excitement about AI fell 14 points in a year (Fast Company). Gamers punish even suspected AI art (itch.io thread; strayspark; itch.io post; itch.io post; Steam forum). The tell-tales are a plastic sheen, drifting details between poses, broken hands and over-smooth symmetric faces (kyndrify; ryla.ai). The current wael_base.png is described as "soft, Pixar-like CGI". (proposal) Steer the material toward soft matte clay (subsurface warmth, less specular gloss), as in the clay references E2–E3 in §9, and make a human retouch pass on face and hands mandatory.

Canvas, anchor and light: the artist's guide already defines the rules

The project's art guide was written for the town, but its character rules fit Wael exactly (village-art-guide.md). These rules are fixed:

  • Canvas: the large character (presenter) canvas is 1024 × 1536 with the anchor at 512, 1472, between the feet. Every pose, frame and layer uses the same canvas, the same anchor pixel and one scale for all characters of a kind.
  • Light: light from the top left "in every image, video and frame", because "the app adds its own shading and effects in this direction". The iOS clear-glass specular rim is also bright top-left, so art and chrome already agree.
  • Content: no text, letters, logos or watermarks. Transparent PNG-32 sRGB, never JPEG for sprites. Shadows stay inside the canvas, and a soft contact shadow is fine.
  • Resolution: generate at 2K/4K and only scale down. Check at 200 % (no AI noise) and at 64 px wide (the silhouette must read).
  • Variants: make each variant by editing an approved image, never by regenerating from scratch (Nano Banana Pro, shown as "Gemini 3.0 Pro" in Scenario, is recommended).
  • Delivery: lowercase underscore names. Finals live in Scenario collections tagged approved.

The town's own canvases follow the same logic (building 1536 × 2048 with the anchor at 768, 1728; prop 1024 × 1024 at 512, 832; 1 tile = 1024 px, drawn at 1/16 on the map) (town README; src/assets/town). The guide's isometric camera rules apply to the town, not to Wael. He is a front or three-quarter presenter. The light rule binds both, so the composite reads as one scene. Inconsistent light direction is the most common failure in 2.5D composites, per the inspiration synthesis in §9.

(proposal) Two refinements apply to this app. First, never trim the transparent margins of a web export, because the anchor must survive so that pose swaps don't jump. Second, export one web size per pose, 512 × 768 WebP (half the master). That covers both the splash slot (about 340 CSS px tall, about 680 device px at 2×) and the 27 % aside slot. Decoded, it costs about 1.6 MB per pose (w × h × 4) (imagecompressor.com). With 12 poses that is about 19 MB if everything is decoded at once, so preload only the current and next screen's poses with img.decode().

Blink and mouth layers stacked on each pose

The guide defines layered characters precisely (village-art-guide.md):

  • <name>_base.png with eyes open and mouth closed
  • _eyes_half, _eyes_closed
  • _mouth_rest (M/B/P), _mouth_a (A/I), _mouth_e (E), _mouth_o (O), _mouth_u (U/W), _mouth_f (F/V)

Every patch is full-canvas and transparent outside the face. Each is made by editing the base ("same image, identical in every way, except the eyes are closed"), and the toggle test allows only the eyes and mouth to change. art.ts already parses exactly these names (art.ts).

The community adds craft rules for avoiding flicker (Spritefy; neolemon):

  • Derive patches from the same render so light and AO match.
  • Feather patch edges by 2–4 px.
  • Keep patches on integer pixel positions.
  • Swap by toggling the opacity of pre-mounted layers rather than changing src, which can blank a frame.

(proposal) Budget the layers by need. Blink layers on every pose (2 patches × 12) are cheap and give the most life per byte. Mouth layers only on the 2–3 talking poses (wael_wave, wael_present, wael_proud), and only once Wael has a real voice (see §5). Blink timing in the local toolkit is 90 ms every 4–7 s (gamification-design.md).

Mid-2026 trend writing describes a return of "physical qualities" (layering, translucency, tactile buttons) after the flat era (Designity; daisyUI: material and dimensional UI; daisyUI 2026 trends). Gestures from video: the guide's route, with the pitfalls named

The guide's route works like this (village-art-guide.md):

  1. Put the start frame on the template over a flat key colour.
  2. Use a video model with start/end control (PixVerse or Kling in Scenario).
  3. Lock the camera. Loops and gestures start and end on the same frame. Keep loops to 1–2 s and gestures to 2.5 s or less ("Every extra second costs memory on the phone").
  4. Deliver the raw MP4 plus the start and end images.
  5. Extract at 12 fps (up to 24 for close-up gestures), key out the colour, and pack.

Reject a clip if the camera moves, the anchor drifts or the subject changes. Kling supports start and end frames up to 4K (Scenario: Kling O1; fal.ai; Eachlabs; ComfyUI node; kling.ai; Luma: Kling 4.0; Futurepedia lesson). Identical start and end frames make a seamless loop point, but an identical image can produce a near-still video. Prompt continuous motion and keep camera instructions minimal (Higgsfield; Higgsfield 2). Scenario's spritesheet recipe renders neutral → action, then swaps the frames to render action → neutral and merges the two, warning that several video models are deprecated (Scenario: spritesheets).

The LayrKits pipeline lists the failure modes (LayrKits; GitHub; X article; TamerinTECH skill):

  • Video models animate wider than the first frame, so leave margins.
  • Chroma key eats green and cyan details.
  • Per-frame recentering fakes motion.
  • Contact with the canvas edge can't be recovered later.

"Veo 3 does not solve the character consistency problem" (winterspeak).

Lip-sync for Tunisian Arabic, if and when Wael speaks

Azure TTS is the only researched option that returns visemes for ar-TN. It offers the voices ar-TN-ReemNeural and ar-TN-HediNeural, and returns viseme IDs only, no blend shapes. What the platforms allow before your splash

Apple says to "Launch instantly". The launch screen should be "nearly identical to the first screen", should "Avoid including text", and "isn't a branding opportunity". If you need a splash, "consider displaying it at the beginning of your onboarding flow", shown "just long enough for people to absorb the information at a glance" (Apple HIG: Launching; Apple HIG: Onboarding). Android 12+ always shows a system splash: an app icon on one opaque colour. An animated icon should stay under 1,000 ms, and a branding image is discouraged. The splash dismisses on the first frame, and an exit animation (for example a 200 ms slide) can bridge into the app. "Perceived startup time is less when viewing an animation" (Android Developers: Splash screens).

Reduced motion, sound and returning users

Under prefers-reduced-motion: reduce, the splash renders the final composite statically: no rise, bob, blink, parallax or chip pop. Pose swaps become instant, or a 150 ms or shorter opacity change, which is an "effect", not motion. The push becomes a fade. Press feedback and progress fills stay, following "reduce, don't remove" (web.dev: prefers-reduced-motion; W3C 2.3.3). An in-app "effects" toggle that defaults to the OS setting is a cheap extra.

Sound: nothing plays on load (the browser wouldn't allow it anyway, and sound-effects.md forbids it). The first CTA tap resumes the single AudioContext that sound.ts owns and plays forward on the same frame.

8. Checklists

Performance

  • [ ] Every continuous animation touches only transform and opacity; no animated filter, box-shadow or blur (web.dev: animations).
  • [ ] will-change added only where a jank was measured, then removed (web.dev).
  • [ ] Pose layers pre-mounted and swapped by opacity; next-screen poses img.decode()d in advance; only the current and next screen's poses held in memory.
  • [ ] Web exports at 2× the CSS box, at most (512 × 768 for Wael); no trimmed margins; decoded-memory total checked (w × h × 4).
  • [ ] Splash first paint needs no JS runtime; splash art ≤ ~200 KB; nothing lazy-loaded blocks the CTA.
  • [ ] Any runtime (Rive, video component) is in a lazy chunk on the game routes only, behind a poster still (Pixelpoint).
  • [ ] At most 1–2 WebGL contexts per screen; Rive canvas or useOffscreenRenderer for several instances (Rive docs).
  • [ ] Everything pauses offscreen (IntersectionObserver), on visibilitychange, and after about 20 s idle.
  • [ ] Video: muted playsinline, HEVC source first in native fallbacks, stacked alpha preferred (Jake Archibald).
  • [ ] Canvas DPR capped at 2; R3F, if ever used: frameloop="demand", PerformanceMonitor, draw calls in the hundreds (R3F docs).
  • [ ] Measured on a real Galaxy A55 and an iPhone via remote debugging (Performance panel), in the webview, in landscape. Not only in the desktop frame.
  • [ ] Works in Safari / WKWebView, not only Chromium.

Accessibility

  • [ ] All motion authored inside prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference; under reduce: static composite, no loops, no parallax, no tilt, no burst (static stamp + sound instead); press feedback kept (web.dev; W3C 2.3.3).
  • [ ] At most one ambient loop per screen, tiny amplitude, no sustained oscillation near 0.2 Hz at large amplitude (Apple HIG: Motion).
  • [ ] Decorative Wael and island: alt="" + aria-hidden; any state a pose shows is also in text and sound.
  • [ ] Text never sits directly on busy art; contrast ≥ 4.5:1 body / ≥ 3:1 large measured on the busiest pixels; Arabic about 2 pt larger.
  • [ ] Glass only on iOS controls, with a 35 % dim over bright art; opaque fallback for reduced transparency / increased contrast (Apple HIG: Materials).
  • [ ] Tap targets ≥ 44 pt / 48 dp including the lip; 3 px teal focus ring outside the lip.
  • [ ] Gyro or tilt only after an explicit tap; never required.
  • [ ] Sound never the only signal; mute toggle reachable; Wael's voice (later) mutable and skippable.
  • [ ] Bubble ≤ 8–12 words, no typewriter, at most one exclamation per screen.

Before shipping an asset

  • [ ] Written consent from Wael on file for the likeness and its uses; human retouch logged.
  • [ ] Canvas 1024 × 1536, anchor between the feet at 512, 1472, within 4 px of the previous pose (village-art-guide.md).
  • [ ] Light from the top left; same camera, lens and rim as the master; colour-graded to the master.
  • [ ] Identity checked side by side with the master on a grid (face, glasses, beard, outfit, sneakers).
  • [ ] Hands correct (count, shape); face not over-smooth; no plastic sheen beyond the style bible.
  • [ ] Gesture vocabulary respected: open palm, no index pointing, no OK sign, no thumbs-up until tested.
  • [ ] Facing correct for its slot; no mirrored render.
  • [ ] Checked at 200 % (no AI noise) and at 64 px wide (silhouette reads).
  • [ ] Flip test against the previous pose and the blink patches: only the eyes and mouth change.

10. Gaps, and what to verify next

The evidence has clear holes, and three of them change decisions.

Community voices are largely missing. Reddit refused the research tool, X returned HTTP 402, LinkedIn returned nothing, and YouTube pages held no transcripts. Opinion in this file therefore comes from forums (itch.io, Framer, Webflow, Steam), blogs and search snippets. Many points in §2 and §4 were not opened directly (community notes: itch.io thread).

Conclusion

The question was never really "3D or flat". It was where depth is allowed to live. In this design, depth lives in the art: renders, contact shadows, a gently layered diorama. The UI keeps the volume it already has, the lip under what you tap. Under that division the owner's wish and the play skin stop contradicting each other. Only seven targeted amendments are needed, not a new design language. The second shift concerns what the 3D Wael is. He is not an illustration to be placed but a small state machine made of stills: a dozen poses, each tied to a moment in the flow, all sharing one canvas, one anchor and one light. The research says that is where his value comes from (gesture and expression, gaze toward the work, reactions to the student). It also means the project's existing artist guide, art.ts and /map/atelier already form most of a character pipeline. They need a character mode in fit.py, not a 3D engine.

The real risks sit outside the render loop: an unconsented or uncanny likeness, a mascot that looks AI-made to a generation primed to distrust that, and a presenter who nags. They are cheap to prevent now (consent, a matte caricature style, a human retouch log, a mute) and expensive to repair after launch. Teach the pipeline on the splash and the five WaelAside screens first. Then let device measurements decide whether clips and a voice earn their bytes.
